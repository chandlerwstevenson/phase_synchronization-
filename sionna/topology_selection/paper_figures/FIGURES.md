# Figures — "Synchronization Topology and Update Protocol Are Not Separable"

All figures for this paper, in one place. Every number traces to
`../RESULTS_A2.md` (the 118-cell pre-registered three-protocol
campaign; simultaneous arm reproduces `../RESULTS_A.md` bit-identically)
or `../RESULTS_A.md` (the original symmetric-protocol study). Plain
matplotlib, no in-figure annotations. Figures 1–2 are rendered from
`../dirA2_cache.json` by `../fig_paper_inversion.py`; the rest were
produced by their study scripts.

Protocols throughout: **simultaneous** (all links correct at once —
the convention in published consensus synchronization), **alternating**
(edge-colored turn-taking, masterless), **directed** (elected-root
tree; each node corrects fully toward its parent). Equal
synchronization airtime within every comparison; N=8, seeds ×3 unless
stated.

---

## fig1_inversion_n8.png — the headline result

Coherent gain for three topologies × three protocols at N=8, full
airtime. This figure shows the protocol *gaps* (the star gains 68
points from simultaneous to directed); note that at full airtime and
N=8 it does **not** show the ranking reversal — the MST stays on top
under every protocol here. The reversal is established separately, at
fixed N=8 and fixed 8.6% airtime with 20 paired seeds (uniform
geometry): under simultaneous updates the MST beats the star
(32.4% vs 23.9%, tree wins 16/20 seeds, sign-test p ≈ 0.012), while
under directed updates the star beats the MST in **18 of 20 seeds**
(92.2% vs 82.2%, p ≈ 0.0004) — the same double reversal holds at
12.8% airtime. A pre-registered replication on the clustered
geometry (10 paired seeds, 2026-08-25) reproduced the simultaneous
half directionally (8/10) but found the directed half **tied**
(5/10) — so the reversal is stated as a uniform-geometry,
fixed-operating-point result. Formally:
G(MST, simultaneous) > G(star, simultaneous) while
G(MST, directed) < G(star, directed), at one array size and one
airtime. The optimal topology's *identity* changes with the protocol,
so "choose the topology first, the algorithm second" is an invalid
design decomposition. (The N=16 version — fig2 — shows the same
reversal at scale, where it is even starker.) Also visible: alternating only partially rescues the star
(38.9%) — sequencing dilutes the hub's per-link service rate and
re-enters the cadence mechanism. (Note: the degree-3 tree instance
here differs from the ad-hoc one in RESULTS_A — disclosed there;
star/MST anchors are exact.)

## fig2_scaling_n16.png — the collapse at N=16

Same comparison at sixteen nodes (5 seeds for star/tree after the
2026-08-25 extension; ring 2 seeds): **every bidirectional protocol
collapses on every topology** (13–30% gain) while the directed
protocol holds 90.2 ± 1.7% (tree) and 97.1 ± 5.2% (star — four of
five seeds at 99.4–99.5, star above tree in 4/5 paired seeds). The
scaling behavior — not just the level — of the array depends on the
protocol, and the only protocol that scales inverts the topology
preference toward exactly the graph the simultaneous convention
rejects.

## dirA2_cadence.png — mechanism 1: cadence (physical)

Gain vs per-link service interval m for the min-variance tree under
all three protocols. The ceiling near m ≈ 2 appears under every
protocol (the driver is oscillator frequency walk between services;
persists at zero frequency offset — the control cells are in
RESULTS_A2), but its severity is protocol-dependent: symmetric falls
84→38%, alternating 68→27%, directed only 96→80%, because
bidirectional protocols amplify branch-ambiguity errors into flip
cascades (32–91 flips/run) that the directed tree confines to one
subtree (3–9 flips).

## dirA2_degree.png — mechanism 2: degree (numerical)

Redesigned (2026-08-25) as a single four-bar staircase on the star
alone — the topology where the mechanism lives. Simultaneous updates
collapse the degree-7 hub to 23.7% (exactly where its linearized
Jacobi iteration diverges — pre-registered spectral radius 1.107);
turn-taking only half-fixes it (38.9%, because serving a 7-link hub
in turns dilutes each link to every-seventh-slot service and
re-enters the cadence mechanism); damping the simultaneous updates or
directing them both fully cure it (92.2% each — and per-seed
*identical*, the linear theory's own fingerprint: both reduce the
iteration's spectral radius below 1). Error bars: standard error,
3 seeds. The sharpened finding: the degree and cadence mechanisms
are coupled, so no per-mechanism patch substitutes for choosing the
protocol correctly.

## dirA2_cycle.png — mechanism 3: cycles (topological)

Chain vs chain-plus-one-chord (ring) under the bidirectional
protocols, 10 seeds (extended 2026-08-25; regenerated by
`../fig_paper_cycle.py`). One chord costs the chain 47 points of
mean gain under simultaneous updates (72.1→24.7%) and 22 under
alternating (72.1→49.7%); the ring's spread is bimodal — the only
cells above 85% gain have residual cycle sums below 0.11 radians,
while cells with cycle sums near ±5 radians stay at or below 42.7%.
Settled impaired states carry
exactly quantized winding numbers — pairwise offsets summing to
integer multiples of 2π around the cycle (±0.001; independently
measured across 24 seeds in the companion testbed,
`../../phase_sync_idea/RESULTS_topology.md` campaign 4). The winding
theory (`../dirA4_winding_theorem.py`, RESULTS_A4) proves this
quantization holds at *every* instant (a residue identity of the
wrapped cycle sum), is conserved under any protocol whose per-step
corrections keep cycle edges away from ±π, and makes wound states
metastable under **every** local update rule: escape requires transit
through an anti-lock event of magnitude π − 2π|w|/n, verified against
the measured transients. (Terminology, precisely: the winding
constraint lives in the measurement graph's cycle space; the flip
ambiguity, by contrast, is a cut-space coset — and the theory's
verified flip rule Δw = −½(sgn r₊ + sgn r₋) quantifies how wrapping
couples the two layers.) Directed protocols use trees and are
structurally exempt.

## fig3_pareto_frontier.png — the coherence/airtime frontier

Coherent gain vs steady-state synchronization airtime (4.3%–30% of
the frame) for all nine (topology, protocol) pairs at N=8, seeds 0–2
(reviewer-requested addition; data RESULTS_A3.md, rendered by
`../dirA3_pareto.py`). The three directed curves sit above the entire
bidirectional band at every airtime level: at 8.6% airtime the best
bidirectional pair delivers 38% gain while star/directed delivers
87.4%, and the cheapest point holding ≥80% gain costs 8.6% airtime
under directed vs 29.9% under any bidirectional protocol — a 3.5×
airtime advantage. The constrained optimum changes with the budget
(chain/directed at 5%, star/directed at 10%, mst/directed at 20%),
restating the joint topology-protocol choice on the airtime axis. One
disclosed anomaly: chain/directed is non-monotone at the lowest two
budgets (round-robin phasing suspected, uninvestigated).

## fig6_protocol_effect.png — same topology, only the protocol differs

The slide-ready statement that the protocol, not the topology,
determines coherent gain (lines = topologies, x = protocol; rendered
from `dirA2_cache.json` by `../fig_paper_protocol_effect.py`). Left
(N=8): under the bidirectional protocols the topology lines scatter
from 23.7% to 83.9% — topology appears to matter enormously — but at
the directed protocol all three lines *converge* to 92–96%: choose
the protocol correctly and the topology choice nearly stops
mattering; the scatter on the left was the protocols' pathologies,
not the graphs'. Right (N=16, now 5 seeds): the purest form of the claim — the
star and min-variance-tree lines lie on top of each other at every
protocol (~16–17% under both bidirectional protocols, 97.1% and
90.2% under directed); the protocol swings gain by ~80 points while
the topology is nearly indistinguishable. This figure shows protocol
*dominance*; the ranking *reversal* between topologies is a separate
claim — see the fig1 caption for its 10-seed fixed-N instantiation.

## fig7_reversal.png — the ranking reversal as crossing lines

The central claim as four bars (fixed N=8, fixed 8.6% sync airtime,
17 common seeds; rendered from the combined caches by
`../fig_paper_reversal.py`). Y-axis: coherent gain. Error bars:
standard error of the mean — the uncertainty of the compared means,
not the population spread (earlier drafts used the population
standard deviation, whose overlap understated the result). Under the
simultaneous protocol the min-variance tree beats the star (31.5% vs
23.8%); under the directed protocol the star beats the tree (93.1%
vs 82.6%). The paired per-seed statistics behind the bars: the same
seed gives both topologies identical deployment, clocks, and
channel, and the star wins 3/17 seeds under simultaneous (sign-test
p = 0.013) versus **16/17 under directed** (p = 0.0003). The
*identity* of the best topology changes with the update protocol, at
one array size and one airtime budget. Mechanism of the flip: bidirectional
protocols punish *degree* (the hub diverges), the directed protocol
punishes *depth* (each hop adds a stale correction under sparse
service) — different protocols bind on different graph properties,
so they prefer opposite graphs. Reconciliation with fig6: at
generous airtime the directed protocol holds every topology near
ceiling and the ordering compresses into noise; the reversal emerges
when airtime (here) or scale (fig2) makes the constraint bind.

## fig4_topologies.png — the measurement graphs themselves

The five N=8 topologies exactly as the campaigns ran them — drawn by
importing the experiments' own graph-builder functions and node
placement (`fig_paper_topologies.py`), so these are the experimental
graphs, not illustrations. Nodes sit at their true deployment
coordinates (meters, 500 m-radius random placement); node labels are
station indices; **each line is one two-way synchronization link** —
the pair that periodically performs the bidirectional exchange whose
half-difference measures that pair's clock offset. The star's hub is
station 3 (the measured degree-7 node). Note the chain panel: it is
an *index-order* chain (0–1–2–…), which criss-crosses geometrically —
faithful to the experiment, and a reminder that link cost follows
distance, not index.

## fig5_protocols.png — what the protocols do on the same graph

The star graph three times, differing only in how corrections are
applied — the paper's central variable made visual. *Simultaneous*:
every link measures and both endpoints correct in the same interval
(the convention of the published consensus literature — and the
configuration that collapses to 23.7%). *Sequential*: the same links
fire in colored turns (proper edge-coloring; on a degree-7 hub this
forces each link to every-seventh-slot service — the cadence
re-entry). *Directed*: arrows show correction flow child→parent
toward the elected root (station 0, filled) — spokes correct toward
the hub, the hub toward the root; the configuration that reaches
99.4% at N=16.

## dirA_gain_vs_budget_uniform.png / dirA_gain_vs_budget_clustered.png — the motivating study

From the original symmetric-protocol selection race (RESULTS_A): gain
vs airtime budget for six topology-selection strategies in two
deployment geometries. These are the plots that exposed the problem —
the estimation-objective selector loses to simple spanning trees
because estimation quality is not what binds under the simultaneous
protocol — and motivated the three-protocol fork above.

---

**Scope for all figures:** waveform-level simulation with full
oscillator and RF impairments; N ≤ 16; one deployment geometry for
the fork campaign; static channel. The linearized theory column
(spectral radii per topology × protocol) is in RESULTS_A2 — it ranks
protocols correctly but is necessary-not-sufficient; the binding
nonlinearity is the modulo-π branch layer.
