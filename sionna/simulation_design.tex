\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This document describes the architecture of the \code{ota\_sync} package, a
sampled-IQ simulation of one-way carrier phase and frequency synchronization
between two radios (master and slave) over an NVIDIA Sionna 3GPP TR~38.901
channel. The slave disciplines its numerically controlled oscillator (NCO)
using only received IQ samples. The simulator models power-law oscillator
noise, a shared reference clock, accumulated timing drift, correlated
shadowing, analog impairments, and correction latency, and grades every
estimate against a noise-free oracle rather than against the receiver's own
measurements. At the default settings the closed-loop residual is
$\approx 70$\,mrad RMS, set by oscillator noise and control latency rather
than by SNR.
\end{abstract}

\section{Overview}
The package provides two models, selected by \code{simulation.py --model}.
The \emph{ideal} model (\code{ota\_sync/core.py}) applies Sionna complex AWGN
directly to an oscillator tone and exists as a baseline. The \emph{SDR} model
(\code{ota\_sync/sdr.py}, the default) is the subject of this document: a
framed preamble is transmitted through a tapped-delay-line multipath channel
and a full analog impairment chain, and the receiver must detect the packet,
acquire timing, estimate carrier frequency offset (CFO) and phase from raw
IQ, and steer its NCO.

Each iteration of the main loop (\code{run\_sdr\_simulation}) represents one
synchronization interval $T$ (default 50\,ms at 1\,MS/s): the oscillators
evolve, one frame is captured and processed, an extended Kalman filter (EKF)
is updated, and a quantized correction is scheduled for the slave NCO.
Defaults place the carrier at 915\,MHz over a stationary TDL-D (line-of-sight)
channel with 100\,ns delay spread at 20\,dB SNR, with an initial CFO of
1500\,Hz.

\section{Oscillator and clock model}
Master and slave oscillators are two-state random walks
(\code{core.Oscillator}) in phase $\phi$ and angular frequency $\omega$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{bmatrix}\phi_{k+1}\\ \omega_{k+1}\end{bmatrix} =
\begin{bmatrix}1 & T\\ 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}\phi_{k}\\ \omega_{k}\end{bmatrix} + \mathbf{w}_k ,
\qquad \mathbf{w}_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\,\mathrm{diag}(\sigma_\phi^2, \sigma_\omega^2)).
\end{equation}
Three further noise processes complete a power-law phase-noise model:
\begin{description}
\item[White FM.] A per-sample phase random walk of standard deviation
$\sigma_{\mathrm{pn}}$ (default $2\times10^{-4}$\,rad) is applied inside each
captured frame. Its end value, plus an independent increment for the dead
time between frames, is folded back into the oscillator state, so intra-frame
and inter-frame phase noise form one continuous process. Over a full interval
of $N_T$ samples this contributes phase variance
$\sigma_{\mathrm{pn}}^2 N_T$ ($\approx 45$\,mrad RMS at defaults).
\item[Flicker FM.] A $1/f$ frequency-noise component is approximated by a
bank of four first-order Gauss--Markov (AR(1)) processes with log-spaced
correlation times spanning the run (\code{\_FlickerFrequencyNoise}); the sum
enters the true relative frequency each interval.
\item[White PM.] Independent per-sample phase jitter (default 5\,mrad) is
applied at the receiver.
\end{description}
Carrier LO and sample clock derive from one reference crystal. Unless a
fixed override is given, the fractional sample-clock error is computed every
interval from the \emph{physical} relative oscillator frequency---the state
minus all accumulated NCO corrections, since digital corrections never touch
the crystal. CFO, sample-clock offset, and frame-timing drift therefore
co-drift, as on real SDR hardware (1500\,Hz at 915\,MHz $\Rightarrow$
1.64\,ppm).

\section{Frame structure and radio link}
\code{make\_sync\_preamble} builds a 4606-sample frame from Zadoff--Chu (ZC)
sequences: a short training field (length-16 ZC repeated 16 times) for
detection and coarse CFO, followed by two cyclic-prefix-protected length-2047
ZC long training fields for fine CFO and phase.

The transmit chain (\code{SDRRadioLink.capture}) modulates the frame onto the
master carrier, applies a soft power-amplifier limiter and DAC quantization,
and passes it through per-interval Sionna TDL channel taps
(\code{cir\_to\_time\_channel} / \code{ApplyTimeChannel}); with nonzero
mobility, Sionna evolves the taps with a Jakes Doppler spectrum. A
log-normal shadowing gain with AR(1) time correlation (2\,dB, 10\,s defaults)
multiplies the taps. The thermal noise floor is fixed once at nominal channel
gain, so \code{snr\_db} is the nominal link SNR and per-frame SNR genuinely
fades.

At the receiver, the clock error accumulates into frame arrival time
($\approx0.08$ samples per interval at defaults). Whole-sample drift steps
the arrival inside the capture window---modeling a receiver that re-centers
its window on each detection---and the sub-sample residual is applied as a
fractional delay in the resampler. The samples are then downconverted by the
slave LO and pass through white-FM/white-PM phase noise, IQ gain/phase
imbalance, AGC, DC offset, and ADC quantization.

\section{Synchronizer}
\code{SDRSynchronizer.estimate} operates on one capture:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Detection and coarse timing:} a normalized autocorrelation at the
short-sequence lag; the correlation angle gives a coarse CFO estimate with
unambiguous range $\pm f_s/(2\cdot16) = \pm31.25$\,kHz.
\item \emph{Fine timing:} cross-correlation of the coarse-corrected window
against the full known preamble; a normalized score below 0.25 declares a
missed detection.
\item \emph{Fine CFO:} the phase of the correlation between the two long
fields, separated by 2175 samples, refines the CFO.
\item \emph{Phase:} after full CFO correction, matched filtering against the
long ZC sequences yields the frame phase referenced to the frame center.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Estimation and control}
An iterated EKF (\code{core.PhaseFrequencyEKF}) tracks the relative state
$[\phi,\omega]$ with observation
$\mathbf{z}=[\cos\phi,\ \sin\phi,\ \omega]^\top$, avoiding phase-wrap
discontinuities; six Gauss--Newton re-linearizations per update handle large
innovations, and the Joseph form keeps the covariance positive semidefinite.
The first detection initializes the state directly (acquisition), since the
coarse+fine CFO estimate must resolve the cycle ambiguity before wrapped-phase
tracking begins.

Both filter covariances are derived from the modeled physics. The measurement
covariance adds three terms per component: the AWGN Cram\'er--Rao bound
($\approx1.1$\,mrad in phase at 20\,dB), the intra-frame white-FM walk
averaged over the long fields ($\approx8$\,mrad, dominant), and the white-PM
contribution. The process covariance adds the white-FM walk over one interval
and the flicker innovation to the oscillator random-walk terms.

Corrections are quantized (16-bit phase, 0.01\,Hz frequency) and load into
the slave NCO only after a configurable processing latency $L$ (default one
interval). The controller knows its latency and forward-predicts each command
through the state transition, as a real design would. Because a command
computed at frame $k$ acts at frame $k{+}L$, the closed loop inherits an
irreducible error from frequency uncertainty propagated over the latency.

\section{Ground-truth oracle and metrics}
A one-way receiver observes oscillator phase \emph{plus} channel phase; the
two are not separately identifiable, so the slave necessarily absorbs a
stable channel-phase bias (about $-2.9$\,rad at defaults), which the raw
oscillator residual metric exposes.

Every error metric is computed against ground truth, never against the
receiver's own measurement. Each capture is also produced along an
\emph{oracle path}: same frame, timing, channel realization, shadowing,
deterministic transmit hardware, and sample-clock timing, but no AWGN, phase
noise, IQ imbalance, DC offset, or ADC quantization. Running the same
synchronizer on the oracle capture defines the true observable OTA phase and
frequency. This matters: grading the EKF against its own input had understated
the phase error by a factor of $\sim$100. Reported quantities are the
tracking error (truth minus EKF estimate), the closed-loop residual (the true
observable phase at each capture, which already reflects every physically
loaded command), and a steady-state RMS masked to intervals after the first
command loads.

\section{Error budget and validation}
The steady-state closed-loop residual decomposes as
\begin{equation}
\sigma^2 \;\approx\; \underbrace{\sigma_{\mathrm{pn}}^2 N_T}_{\text{white-FM walk per interval}}
\;+\; \underbrace{\big(\sigma_{\hat\omega}\, L\, T\big)^2}_{\text{freq.\ uncertainty} \times \text{latency}}
\;+\; \underbrace{\sigma_{\mathrm{track}}^2}_{\text{measurement/tracking}},
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_{\hat\omega}$ is the EKF steady-state frequency posterior. The
simulation confirms each term in isolation (100 iterations, defaults unless
noted):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Configuration & Tracking RMSE & Closed-loop residual RMS & Dominant term \\
\midrule
Full defaults            & 12.0\,mrad & 69.6\,mrad & walk ($\approx45$) $\oplus$ latency ($\approx38$) \\
No RF impairments        & 1.1\,mrad  & 34.3\,mrad & latency only \\
Zero correction latency  & 12.3\,mrad & 12.3\,mrad & tracking only \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
Detection is 100\% in all cases; frequency tracking RMSE is
$\approx0.2$\,Hz. The practical implication is that beyond moderate SNR the
achievable phase lock is set by oscillator quality, sync interval, and
control latency---not preamble energy.

\section{Coherent collaboration modes}
Two architectures build on the sync machinery (\code{ota\_sync/coherent.py}),
matching the two ways stations can collaborate coherently.

\emph{CSI-aided joint transmission} (\code{--csi-gain}): when a user feeds
back channel estimates, any static per-station phase bias---including the
one-way channel-phase bias---is absorbed by CSI, so combining gain depends
only on the \emph{differential} phase drift between refreshes. Evaluated from
the one-way run's closed-loop residual, the two-station gain at the user
stays above 99.5\% even with CSI refreshed only once per second.

\emph{Open-loop coherence for passive detection} (\code{--model twoway}):
with no feedback path, the inter-station channel phase must be physically
removed. A reciprocal two-way exchange---forward and reverse frames traverse
the same channel realization and shadowing state---cancels it: the
half-difference of the two directional phase measurements observes the pure
oscillator offset, making absolute carrier alignment identifiable. At
defaults this yields an 82\,mrad steady-state oscillator residual (versus the
$-2.9$\,rad unresolvable bias of the one-way loop) and a mean two-station
open-loop coherent gain $\cos^2(\phi_e/2)$ of 99.8\%. Two limits are modeled
honestly: the half-difference carries a global $\pi$ ambiguity (resolved in
hardware by a one-time combining check), and residual TX/RX chain asymmetry
(\code{twoway\_chain\_asymmetry\_deg}) does not cancel in a two-way exchange
and must come from loopback calibration.

\section{Usage}
\begin{verbatim}
python -m pip install -r requirements.txt   # sionna-no-rt==2.0.1
python simulation.py --plot                 # phase acquisition and residual
python simulation.py --plot-all             # full radio diagnostics
python simulation.py --snr-db 8 --cfo-hz 10000 --tdl-model C
python simulation.py --correction-latency 0 --no-rf-impairments
python simulation.py --model twoway         # open-loop coherence (reciprocal)
python simulation.py --csi-gain             # JT gain vs. CSI refresh cadence
python -m pytest                            # 11 tests, incl. oracle consistency
\end{verbatim}
Key switches: \code{--sfo-ppm} forces a fixed sample-clock offset instead of
the shared-reference derivation; \code{--flicker-std-hz} and
\code{--shadowing-std-db} scale the flicker and shadowing processes;
\code{--model ideal} runs the AWGN baseline.
